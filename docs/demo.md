# See the skill in action

This is an authored, **synthetic demonstration**, not a recorded user session or proof of live Claude/ChatGPT performance. Every career detail below is fictional.

## From an overlooked task to a useful story

**Starting point**

> “I just made a handoff checklist. Maya led the launch. I want a design operations role, but I don't know what I have to show.”

**What Career Vault investigates**

The skill asks one question at a time about what the person changed, who used it and what happened. The supplied evidence in this example is:

> “I created the checklist. Our team used it to catch missing assets before client delivery. We didn't measure time saved or revenue.”

**Assessment for review**

> “This gives us a concrete contribution to a shared working practice. For design operations, it may be worth leading with. The evidence supports creating the checklist and its use by the team; broader operations ownership and measured savings remain unestablished.”

The person agrees to lead with this accomplishment. The record links it to claim K1 and labels the source as personal recollection.

**Three uses of the same fact**

| Material | Evidence-grounded content |
| --- | --- |
| Resume | Created a handoff checklist the team used to catch missing assets before client delivery. |
| Case study | I created the checklist for reviewing assets before handoff. Maya led the wider launch. Our team used the checklist to catch missing assets; time and revenue impact were not measured. |
| Presentation | Context: client handoff. My role: checklist creation. Team role: use. Outcome: catching missing assets before delivery. Limits: no measured savings. |

**What stays in the record**

The source account, individual/team attribution, missing evidence, approved claim, output versions and next questions all remain—even when the resume uses only one sentence. In a complete interview, this project sits within the last-three-company coverage rather than replacing it.
