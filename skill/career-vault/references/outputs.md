# From evidence to useful materials

Read the claim register and agreed assessment before drafting. If approval is missing, discuss proposed positioning and top accomplishment bullets first. Urgent partial drafts must visibly identify unresolved scope and approval; never label them final. The portable record is the source; outputs are selections, not replacements.

Use [output-templates.md](../assets/output-templates.md) for shape. Adapt language and length to the person's voice, audience, job description and requested format. Never change actual job titles to the target role. A target headline must be clearly separate from job history. Include only supported skills; do not claim missing experience to match a job posting.

## Resume

Agree the strongest accomplishment bullets per company/project with the user. Write personal action + context + supported value/outcome. When quantitative proof is unavailable, use specific qualitative evidence. Keep team credit accurate. Do not invent numbers or put unresolved placeholders into a final resume. Gaps can be discussed in review notes outside the public-facing draft. Avoid presenting an inferred outcome as a measured result. Preserve broader notes and unused accomplishments in the record.

## Written case studies

Create these when relevant projects exist; record a defer/no-project decision otherwise. Establish context, constraints, individual role, collaborators, decisions/actions and outcomes with limitations. Distinguish design intent from observed effect. Include less visible work if it demonstrates value. Keep internal source/claim notes for review; strip them from a public version only after approval without stripping the factual grounding from the record.

## Portfolio and website

Turn approved positioning into a short introduction and project summaries. Every promise about ability needs evidence. Propose navigation and content only as requested; do not publish or build a site automatically. Redact client details when required.

## Cover letter

Use a specific opportunity, supported reasons for fit and two relevant examples. Express motivation in the person's language; do not invent personal passion, relationships or knowledge of the employer. Ask for a missing motivation rather than manufacturing one.

## Presentation

Ask about audience, purpose and time if not known. Reuse the case study's evidence in a concise arc: problem, role, choices, result and relevance. Speaker notes can retain nuance that would clutter slides. A text outline is an honest deliverable when slide-file tools are unavailable; do not claim a PowerPoint exists. File creation depends on the host's tools and user request.

## Updates

Show an impact table when a claim or goal changes: output/version, old emphasis or affected claim, proposed revision, review needed. A new case study may warrant changing the resume highlights. A changed goal changes selection and emphasis, not historical facts. Disputed claims must be removed from final-use drafts until resolved. Keep old output versions labeled outdated rather than silently replacing approved text.

Before delivery: compare every substantive assertion to source/claim IDs; check individual/team boundaries, metrics, dates, title accuracy, confidentiality, tone and user approval. Provide the full updated record alongside requested materials and distinguish written content from files actually saved.
